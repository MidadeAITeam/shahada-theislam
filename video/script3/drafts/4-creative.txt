THE LAST MESSAGE  |  «آخِرُ رسالة»
A 2-minute film for theislam.chat | After the Shahada (ما بعد الشهادة)
Draft 4 — creative lens
=====================================================================

CENTRAL CONCEPT
One screen and one blinking cursor. The film opens on a chat window where
someone has just typed the Shahada. We pull back to show the whole
platform, then come back to the same cursor and see the silence that
followed in so many real conversations. The twist: the film shows the
SAME screen a second time, and this time something answers. The opening
image comes back changed.
Central metaphor: the Shahada as the "last message" in the chat log. The
film's job is to turn the last message into the first lesson.
Final line (the one people repeat):
  «كانت آخِرَ رسالة… فصارت أوّلَ درس.»
  "It was the last message. Now it is the first lesson."

STAGING NOTE (truthfulness)
The cold-open chat is the real Shahada-moment footage from the platform,
with the user's text anonymised. "Three in the morning" is staging. Keep
the small caption "مشهد تمثيلي | Dramatised" on screen for beats 1–2 and
show no real names or identifying details. Every number below is taken
exactly from the brief, and the film computes no derived numbers.

NARRATION WORD COUNT & TIMING
=====================================================================
Arabic narration (beats 1–4, 6–12): 223 words (counted by script, excluding stage directions)
At ~2.2 words/s that is ~102 s of voice, plus ~7 s of written pauses and silences
(0:00–0:04 cold open, 1 s after «الحمد لله», 2 × 1 s in beat 7, the beat-12 pause)
plus the 8 s testimonial (beat 5), which gives ~117 s in total, inside the 120 s limit.
Beat timings above are targets. Lock them to the final ElevenLabs read.
If the read still runs long, cut in this order:
(a) «ثمّ يختارُ هو: الوضوء، أو الصلاة، أو الفاتحة» (keep the choice screen as a visual; saves ~3 s);
(b) «في تلك الليلة،» in beat 12 (saves ~1 s; the line still works);
(c) «الحمدُ لله» stays. Never cut it.

Voice direction: a low, close, unhurried male documentary voice. Beats 1–2 are nearly a whisper.
Beats 3–4 are warm. Beat 7 is flat and quiet, with no drama added; the numbers carry the weight.
Beat 8 takes a full breath before «ألّا». In beat 12, hold the pause before «فصارت» for a full second.

=====================================================================
WHY THIS WILL HOLD A MILLION VIEWERS
=====================================================================
1. The first frame is a puzzle. A blinking cursor in the dark, with someone typing, makes viewers
   wait to see what is being written. When they read the Shahada, they are already inside the scene.
2. One image carries the whole film. The same screen appears three times: as a miracle (beat 1),
   as silence (beat 6–7) and as an answer (beat 9). Viewers feel the problem and the solution because
   they see the same frame change.
3. The numbers become something people feel. 166 gold lights, 89 of them going out in silence and
   11 left, turn a data export into a loss viewers can see. Every figure is exact, so the emotion
   rests on what really happened and nothing is exaggerated.
4. The sound design follows the story. The film begins in silence, the hum rises with the platform's
   reach and stops when the problem appears. The first "answer" chime comes only when the journey
   card arrives, so the audience hears the moment of relief.
5. The final line is short and works on its own. «كانت آخِرَ رسالة… فصارت أوّلَ درس» is seven words,
   easy to remember and quote, and sums up the whole project.
