JUDGE REVIEW (skeptical, busy) - 27 slides

FIRST IMPRESSION (3 lines)
1. Strong, honest problem framing (166 shahadas, 89 dropped, no lesson to give) and a rare, genuinely candid evaluation; a real product on a real platform, grounded in the book "Al-Wajiz".
2. But it is dense: slides 13-20 read like a technical report, and the key claim (it helps new Muslims) is unmeasured, which the deck itself admits.
3. Credible and safe-by-design (checker, referral to humans); risk is that I leave remembering numbers and architecture, not the learner's experience.

5 QUESTIONS THE DECK DOES NOT ANSWER
1. Who is the Shariah reviewer, and how many of the 19 lessons are actually signed off today? Slides say "pending" but never give a count (e.g. 5/19). That is the core trust claim.
2. Who staffs the mentors after the challenge? Slide 23 says "Asul team takes over" - how many mentors, in which languages, with what response time? A bot that sends users to an empty inbox is worse than none.
3. Why does the product depend on a single source (Al-Wajiz)? What happens with madhhab differences, or a question the book is silent on, beyond "sorry"? Slide 19 shows 91% apology vs 100% needed on sensitive referral (81%): what do the 19% of sensitive questions get that is not referral, concretely?
4. Is there any evidence that the 11 who left contact details / the 13 who returned would increase? What is the target retention metric, and how will you measure it ethically without reading chats?
5. Gemini/OpenAI dependency and data: user disclosures about religion go to third-party model providers. What is the retention/consent/legal position (GDPR, users in restrictive countries where conversion is dangerous)?

3 SLIDES THAT WOULD LOWER MY SCORE
- Slide 19/20: too many numbers (20+ rows), mixed metrics, with 81% on the most sensitive category and "improved after peeking" caveats; a judge cannot tell what is the headline. The honesty is good but the density hides it. Needs one headline row and 3 supporting.
- Slide 16: model/tool inventory table with version strings (gemini-3.5-flash-lite etc.) is unreadable on screen and adds little; sounds like name-dropping; unexplained fallbacks.
- Slide 26: shows a plaintext demo password on a slide that will be public/recorded; also the 3-minute path is crammed in a footer. Move credentials to the submission form/README; show a QR code.
(Honourable mention: slide 25 team slide has placeholder labels "الاسم", "عضو" and no real names for Mohamed/others in roles beyond one line - looks unfinished.)
